What's new: November 2026

November 1 to 9 · 120 changes

November brought tournaments, a computer opponent that talks back, pre-moves and post-quantum accounts.

Highlights

  • Tournaments: anyone signed in can run a round robin, Swiss or arena for up to 100 players, with standings and an overall leaderboard.
  • PigeonAI: a computer opponent run by the players' own devices together, which chats (and trash-talks) during the game.
  • Pre-moves and dragging: queue your next move during the opponent's turn, and drag pieces with the piece under your finger.
  • Accounts: post-quantum, signed accounts that work on any device; your games are signed and kept in a room of your own.
  • Games survive reloads: a reload mid-game picks the game back up, against a person or the computer.
  • Share a game: a link to any finished game, opened by anyone, with the moves checked against your signed copy.

Playing

The board got pre-moves, dragging and better sound, and the lobby got simpler.

  • Pre-moves: during the opponent's turn, pick up a piece and choose any square it could reach, recaptures included; it is played the moment your turn comes, if still legal. Tap or right-click to take it back.
  • Dragging: drag a piece with mouse, finger or pen; it follows the pointer.
  • One Play panel: a Someone/Computer toggle, a difficulty slider, and your last time control remembered. Any plays the first free opponent at the time control of whoever started looking first (10+0 by default, set in Settings).
  • Reloads: a reload mid-game picks the game back up, against a person or the computer; a finished game stays finished. A new version of the site waits until you are back in the lobby before it loads.
  • Sounds: a snap on check, a different knock for wins, losses and draws, and the opponent's moves are heard on iPhone and iPad too. If an iPad keeps sound off, a Tap for sound button turns it on.
  • Notifications: "Your move" and "Your game started" when you are away, on by default in the installed app.
  • Look: new dark (Bluewood) and light (Cruise) themes; captured pieces are bigger, outlined in dark mode, and repeats overlap.
  • Move list and chat always scroll to the latest line.
  • Fair clocks: a win on time counts only once the opponent's page confirms the position.

PigeonAI

PigeonAI is a new choice under Play → Computer, next to Standard (Stockfish): a language model run by the players' own devices together, and a bit of a jerk.

  • Run by everyone: each device with a GPU holds a share of the model (up to 2.5 GB on a computer, 250 MB on a phone or tablet), fetched from other players and PigeonFS. A progress bar shows this device's share loading.
  • Always a legal move: the model picks among the legal moves, and never shuffles a piece back and forth.
  • Chat: talk to it during the game. It answers as a cocky top player, knows the game (who is ahead, the last moves, chess slang), and streams its reply as it writes.
  • Speaks up on its own: always after it takes one of your pieces ("Took your rook."), and now and then after its other moves, after a short, varying pause.
  • Kept across reloads: the chat and the game come back after a reload.

Accounts, sharing and history

Accounts are now signed, post-quantum records that follow you to any device, and your games are yours.

  • Post-quantum accounts: keys pair today's P-256 with ML-DSA-65 and ML-KEM-768; passwords are sealed with Argon2id. Older accounts move over at their next login, keeping their name.
  • Any device: log in elsewhere and the account arrives from your relay or other players; a name can only be registered once.
  • Stay signed in: sign-in stays on a device until you log out, encrypted with a key the browser keeps to itself.
  • Names in games: your opponent's signed-in name shows on the board.
  • Your games, signed: each finished game is signed by your account and kept, unchangeable, in a storage room of your own on PigeonFS.
  • Share a game: Copy link at the end of a game or in History copies a link anyone can open; the page says whether the moves match your signed copy. Shared links show their own preview in chats and social apps.

Fixes

The biggest fix: the page no longer freezes at the end of a game.

  • The page froze at the end of a game in some browsers; the board no longer redraws itself forever.
  • A move that ends the game reaches the opponent at once.
  • No false game endings after a page was paused in the background.
  • Safari connections no longer drop every 20 seconds.
  • The Standard computer never stalls on a missing engine answer.
  • A promotion reaches the other board as the piece chosen (a bare pawn push was read as a knight).
  • Reloading after finishing a game against the computer no longer brings it back one move short and unfinished.
  • No more repeating console messages.
